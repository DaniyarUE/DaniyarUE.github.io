HIGGSFIELD · GROWTH MANAGER · ALMATY

How I'd fill the creator pipeline in 30 days.

I'm applying for the Growth Manager role in Almaty. Instead of telling you how I'd work, here's me doing it. I wrote this from outside the company, so some of it is probably wrong. Tell me which part.

01 · WHAT I FOUND

The acquisition machine already exists.

First I went looking for what's already running. Three things are live. Creator Partnership Program: full plan, monthly credits, early access to models, partner community. No follower minimum, every application read by a person. Higgsfield Earn: self-serve, connect Instagram, YouTube or X, take a campaign, get paid per approved video with bonuses at view milestones. And B2B and studio deals.

So nobody needs me to build creator acquisition. It's built. The job is to feed it.

That moves where the work is. In programs like this the number that hurts is not signups. It's the drop between accepted and second video. People join, take the credits, post one test, go quiet. If the KPI is unique videos per month, that's where the month is won or lost.

I could be wrong from the outside. Four numbers would tell me: applications, accepted, first video posted, second video. If the drop is before acceptance, the job is recruiting and I'd rewrite this page. If it's after, the job is activation. I'd ask for those on day one.

02 · WHERE I'D LOOK

Five segments, five different offers

Same product, five different trades. One offer for everyone is how you end up with a pile of signups and no videos.

01

People already posting AI video every week

The base of the list

Not the biggest accounts. The ones who publish weekly and whose comments are full of "what tool is this". They already have the habit, so I'm not asking them to change how they work, only what they work with.

Offer: Earn, plus a brief with three hooks so the first video takes 20 minutes
02

Tool reviewers and tutorial channels

Highest output per person

Their format is "new model dropped, here's what it does". Every release hands them a video for free, so they keep publishing without me chasing them. And their viewers are the people who actually pay for the tool.

Offer: access before release, embargo, they post the day it goes live
03

Motion designers and VFX people moving into AI

Reaches the sceptics

Half the internet still thinks AI video looks cheap. Those people don't listen to AI creators. They listen to someone with craft. One honest breakdown from this group does more there than ten promos.

Offer: CPP plus a breakdown we make together, their framing not ours
04

Small brands and agency owners making their own ads

Nobody is chasing them

This is the one I'd push hardest, because I'm in it. I run an AI content studio in Dubai and I've sold this exact thing to brands: a full product catalogue rebuilt in 24 hours, videos that then ran as live Meta ads. They publish constantly and they tag what they use. Nobody recruits them because they don't look like influencers. I have the network and I know the pitch, because I used it on myself.

Offer: business plan plus a case study they co-sign
05

AI filmmakers with a festival record

Prestige, not distribution

Worth having and worth not overrating. An award is credibility, not reach. Most of them finish two films a year and their audiences are small. I'd take a few for the brand, not for the KPI, and I'd keep that number small. This is the segment I'd cut first if the month was going badly.

Offer: CPP, early access, support around festival submissions

04 · THE OUTREACH

What I'd actually send

Short. One ask. No flattery. The only part that changes is the first line, and if I can't write something true and specific there, that person shouldn't be on the list.

Hi [name] — [one true, specific line about something they made]. I run creator partnerships at Higgsfield. We build AI video models. The part that matters to you: partners get a full plan with monthly credits and access to models before they're public. No posting quota, no approval over what you make. If you use it and it's good, tag us. Worth 15 minutes?

Then I'd test it. Four versions of that first line, fifty sends each, check reply rate, keep the one that works and drop the rest. Same loop I ran on the YouTube channel. Different hooks, same method.

05 · FIRST MONTH

How I'd spend the first month

  1. Get the numbers, build the list. The four funnel numbers, and whatever the current baseline for videos per month is. Then the list: candidates across the five segments, scored on the filter above, each with its line. Nothing sent yet.
  2. Send in batches, with variants. Several versions of the opening line, split across segments. The point isn't volume. It's finding out which segment answers and which message works, before spending the month on the wrong one.
  3. Push the yeses to a posted video. This is the actual job. Remove every step between "sure" and "posted": brief, hooks, credits already in the account. Anyone who goes quiet, I ask why. That answer is worth more than the next hundred sends.
  4. Report, then cut. What it cost to get one posted video, by segment. Reply rate by variant. How many came back for a second video. Then drop whatever didn't produce and put the next month into whatever did.

I've left targets out on purpose. I don't know your baseline or what you'd call a good month, and a number invented from outside is noise. Tell me where it is now and I'll tell you what I think I can move it to, and what I'd need.
